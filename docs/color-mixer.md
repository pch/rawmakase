# Color mixer and color grading

## Measurement

Camera Raw 18.6 rendered every color mixer slider at ±100, and Saturation and Vibrance at ±50, on nine photos (Fujifilm X100F, Sony A7 II and A7CR, chosen to cover skin, foliage, sky and magenta). Color grading's earlier tables were rendered for shadows, midtones, highlights and global at six hues (Saturation 50) plus Saturation 100 and Luminance ±50, on four photos; its current curves come from the synthetic chart (below). `scripts/camera-raw-sweep.py` produces the renders and `scripts/lightroom-scorecard.py` scores them.

## Color mixer, Saturation and Vibrance

Their effect on the default rendering is a hue/saturation/value lookup in linear ProPhoto RGB. The change depends on hue, saturation and brightness. `crates/rawmakase-engine/src/develop/color_mixer.rs` applies measured tables (36 hues × 6 saturations × 6 values, hue shift and log2 saturation/value factors per slider extreme), interpolated trilinearly. The first tables, measured on the photos above (`color_mixer.bin`), are no longer rendered: the [chart tables](#chart-tables) replaced them. Slider position scales the hue shift, the value factor's log and positive saturation's log linearly. Negative saturation scales the saturation factor itself linearly: Camera Raw's response at −25 and −50 is close to linear in the factor, and scaling the log of a strong measured desaturation removed up to twice as much color (Blue −25 on one photo lowered mean saturation by 0.050 instead of Camera Raw's 0.024). Several sliders add their changes.

Checked with Camera Raw 18.6 on three photos (X100F ×2, A7 II) for single bands at −25/−50/−100/+50, all bands at −25/−50/+50, a pair and one real multi-band edit. Mean extra error over each photo's default render, before → after: all bands −50 +0.0019 → −0.0001, all bands −25 +0.0023 → 0.0000, the multi-band edit +0.0018 → +0.0001, Orange −50 +0.0014 → +0.0001; positive sliders and −100 unchanged. The Lightroom X100F scorecard went from 0.0093 to 0.0090 (HSL reference 0.0178 → 0.0113, portrait mix 0.0152 → 0.0120), with no reference worse. With each photo left out of the fit, every slider reproduces Camera Raw to 0.0002–0.0066 MAE, 3–5× closer than leaving the image unchanged. On three of the photos, the extra error over the default render drops from +0.0022 to +0.0003 on average, and for the worst slider (Orange Luminance −100) from +0.0153 to +0.0016. Blue and purple are the least covered bands.

### Chart tables

The band tables are refitted to Camera Raw 18.7 on a dense synthetic chart (`color_mixer_chart.bin`). The chart has 1,728 colors (72 hues every 5° in Oklab, at six lightnesses and four fractions of the most chroma sRGB holds there) and a gray ramp; Camera Raw rendered it with every band's Hue, Saturation and Luminance at ±100, and Blue and Purple also at ±50 (`scripts/corpus/color-mixer.py`). The fit takes Camera Raw's default render as the mixer's input. Cells the chart does not reach, such as colors beyond sRGB, keep the photo tables' values; Saturation and Vibrance are unchanged. What the renders showed:

- No band's slider moves a gray, while the photo tables moved one by up to 1.5% (Red Luminance ±100). The chart tables leave grays alone.
- Luminance is not linear in the slider: −50 makes about a third of −100's change in log value and +50 about 58% of +100's, on Blue and Purple alike. The chart tables scale band Luminance by the slider position to the power 1.6 when darkening and 0.79 when lightening. Hue and Saturation keep the scaling above.

On the chart's patches left out of the fit (every other hue), the mean ΔE00 over the 60 rendered slider settings goes from 0.50 with the photo tables to 0.15, and no setting gets worse; Blue Luminance −100 goes from 1.40 to 0.54, Purple Luminance −100 from 1.60 to 0.27, Blue Hue −100 from 0.91 to 0.27. On the corpus chart, every case with a mixer slider is closer to Camera Raw (the default render is 0.66): the single-band `hue-*` cases sit at 0.66–0.79 (were 0.69–1.39), `saturation-*` at 0.66–1.02 (0.72–1.35), `luminance-*` at 0.64–1.31 (0.82–2.13), and the combined `mixer-*` cases at 0.75–1.42 (0.90–3.07).

### Saturation

Camera Raw 18.7's Saturation −100 turns every color into a gray of its own luminance (Rec. 709 Y in linear sRGB, within 3% on the corpus chart), where the photo tables, measured at ±50 and extrapolated, left some color (`saturation-100`: mean ΔE00 3.97, p95 15.5). RAWmakase keeps the photo tables down to −50 and fades from there to the color's luminance, reaching it at −100: `saturation-100` is now 0.65 (p95 1.8), and everything from −50 up renders as before.

On the chart, Camera Raw's whole slider behaves as a blend toward luminance, out = Y + k (rgb − Y) with k 0, 0.52, 1.45 and 1.85 at −100, −50, +50 and +100, which reproduces its renders from its own default to mean ΔE00 0.42–0.97. Rendering ±50 that way was worse on real photos, though: on 20 pixls.us photos at Saturation +50 (not used for any fit) the median went from 1.18 to 1.47, and on our own photos at ±50 from 0.64 to 0.79. So only the fade to gray is used. Vibrance is not such a blend: its strength varies with hue as well as chroma ([vibrance](#vibrance)).

### Vibrance

Camera Raw 18.7's Vibrance is far from linear in the slider: on the dense chart, −100 removes most of a color's saturation (mean log2 saturation factor −2.3) where −50 removes much less (−0.78), and the photo tables, measured at ±50 and extrapolated, missed −100 badly (`vibrance-100`: mean ΔE00 3.90, p95 10.5). Camera Raw rendered the chart at ±25, ±50, ±75 and ±100 (`scripts/corpus/color-mixer.py`'s chart), and a table fitted at each position reproduces the patches left out of the fit 3–5 times closer than the photo tables (−100: 3.42 → 0.64, +100: 1.37 → 0.44, −50: 1.17 → 0.33). On our own photos at ±50, though, the chart tables were a little further from Camera Raw (mean 0.75 → 0.80) than the photo tables measured there, so RAWmakase keeps the photo tables up to ±50 and move to the chart's tables at ±75 and ±100, interpolating between positions. On the corpus chart `vibrance-100` is now 0.83 (p95 1.9) and `vibrance+100` 1.00, down from 1.54; ±50 renders as before.

Both the mixer and grading run after the tone curves (basic curves and point curves). With them before the point curve, Lightroom references that combine grading with a faded point curve scored worse (e.g. global blue 0.0206, against 0.0083 after).

## Color grading

**Measurement.** Camera Raw 18.7 rendered the synthetic chart (`synthetic-d65`) with about 740 settings, read in 16-bit ProPhoto RGB (`scripts/corpus/color-grading.py`): each region at twelve hues and Saturation 25, 50, 75 and 100; Shadows, Midtones and Highlights at two hues over Blending 0–100 (steps of 25) and Balance −100 to +100 (steps of 12 or 13); every Luminance slider at ±25, ±50, ±75 and ±100; and 13 held-out combinations. What they show:

- Color grading is a curve per channel of linear ProPhoto RGB. A channel's output depends on that channel's input only: applying the gray ramp's three curves to every patch reproduces Camera Raw to 0.1–0.2 ΔE00, while the same curves in sRGB, Display P3, Rec. 2020 or ACES AP1 primaries leave 0.6–4.4, and curves driven by the pixel's luminance (the earlier operator's model) 0.7–1.0.
- A region's curves are gains of the form 1 + A₁(x)·p₁ + A₂(x)·p₂ (three terms for Global): profiles A along the channel value, and per-channel coefficients p that only hue and saturation set. Shadows' gains are close to linear in the tint color with ProPhoto luminance kept, until a channel would go below about 6% of its value, where Camera Raw stops it and lifts the others instead; Midtones, Highlights and Global act on log gains. Regions multiply.
- Blending and Balance reshape the profiles and leave the coefficients alone. Balance moves the regions along the tones; Blending widens them and strengthens Shadows a little. Global ignores both. A legacy split-toning record (SplitToning keys without ColorGrade keys) renders exactly as Blending 100.
- Luminance is one curve for all channels, applied before the tint (the other order, or multiplying the two, left more error). Darkening scales a channel's value; lifting adds in encoded values. Under other Blending or Balance a Luminance slider has the strength it has at the default where the region's profile has the same relative weight.
- A look's split toning is a grading pass of its own, after the user's, at Blending 100: merged with the user's grading (the user's region winning, as RAWmakase did) it left 3.8 ΔE00 on `look-split-grading-shadows-h30` with Camera Raw's own default render as input, a second pass 1.6.

**Rendering.** `crates/rawmakase-engine/src/develop/color_grade_curves.rs` builds three gain curves (64 samples of x^(1/2.2)) for an edit, from the profiles interpolated over Blending and Balance and the coefficients interpolated over hue (30° steps) and saturation, then grades each channel of linear ProPhoto RGB after the color mixer, Point Color and a look's RGB table. The GPU preview applies the same curves. With Camera Raw's own default render as input, the fit reproduces the fitting renders to mean ΔE00 0.26 (hue and saturation), 0.21 (Blending and Balance) and 0.12 (Luminance), and the held-out combinations to 0.13–0.6, except strong tints at Blending near 100: Shadows and Highlights both at Saturation 60 and Blending 100 leave 2.5, Highlights at Saturation 85 and Blending 90 1.3, as Camera Raw stops a darkened channel earlier than the fitted curves. Luminance lifts of Highlights under other Blending or Balance leave about 0.6.

On the corpus chart, RAWmakase's mean ΔE00 from Camera Raw, before → after: Shadows H30 3.06 → 1.15 and H210 2.59 → 1.07, Midtones H30 2.15 → 0.99 and H210 1.88 → 0.96, Highlights H30 4.15 → 1.80 and H210 4.05 → 1.05, Blending 100 3.37 → 1.58, Balance +50 4.00 → 2.02, the split-toning look at 50/100/200% 1.62/2.55/3.46 → 1.05/1.20/2.01, with the user's shadows 3.46 → 2.17, grading under an RGB-table look 2.03/3.13 → 0.75/1.22; the default render is 0.91. Most of what remained was in saturated bright colors the tint pushes out of sRGB; RAWmakase now clips those per channel as Camera Raw does ([out-of-gamut colors](color-pipeline.md#out-of-gamut-colors)), which brings the `grading-*` cases to 0.56–1.43.

**Earlier operator.** Before the measured curves, grading used per-channel gains driven by the pixel's luminance, measured with Camera Raw 18.6 on four photos at the default Blending (50) and Balance (0) for six hues at Saturation 50 plus Saturation 100 and Luminance ±50, and RAWmakase's first approximation for other Blending and Balance and for legacy split toning, with a look's split toning merged into the user's grading. It is no longer rendered: edits made with it render with the curves.

**Panel.** As in Lightroom, the panel shows the Shadows, Midtones and Highlights wheels together (3-Way) or one wheel large, Global included. Dragging a wheel's puck sets hue and saturation in whole degrees and percent, as Lightroom stores them; Shift keeps a drag to hue or to saturation, whichever it moves along first, and Cmd (Ctrl elsewhere) moves the puck a tenth as far. A plain click or drag away from the puck moves it under the pointer. Double-clicking a wheel resets its hue and saturation. Every number can be typed. The wheels change the same values the sliders did, so an edit renders identically however it was set. Each drag is one History step named as Lightroom names them ("Shadow Hue", "Highlight Saturation", or "Shadow Hue & Saturation" when the puck moved both ways, which is RAWmakase's own name: no such step was found in a Lightroom catalog to copy).

## Point Color

**Format.** Camera Raw 18.7 stores each swatch as one item of `crs:PointColors` with 19 numbers, and its Variance as the matching item of `crs:ColorVariance`: the sampled color (HSV hue in sixths of a turn, 0–6; saturation; value, linear), the Hue, Saturation and Luminance shifts (−1 to 1), Range (0–1, 0.5 by default), then the hue, saturation and luminance ranges, four points each (outer, inner, inner, outer). Hue points are 0–1 across the swatch's hue window with the swatch at 0.5; saturation points are on HSV saturation; luminance points are on sRGB-encoded value. A sampled swatch gets the hue points 0, ⅓, ⅔, 1 and saturation and luminance points 0.73 and 0.18 either side of its own (clipped to 0–1), as Adobe's shipped presets show. Camera Raw drops a swatch with a hue of 6 or more, a hue range without inner span, or saturation or luminance ranges whose inner points don't hold the sampled color; Lightroom writes an empty selection as 19 values of −1 with a Variance of −50. RAWmakase reads swatches from presets, sidecars and the catalog (as quoted strings or bare numbers; no active swatch was found in a real catalog), drops the ones Camera Raw drops, keeps at most eight, and writes them back in Camera Raw's form. Presets with the Color group write Lightroom's empty selection when the photo has none, so applying them clears swatches.

**Measurement.** The operator was fitted to Camera Raw 18.7 renders of three synthetic DNG charts (no Adobe data): dense grids of hue, saturation and value in linear ProPhoto RGB, with single swatches varying each shift, Range, Variance and every range, swatches at red, yellow, green, blue and magenta, low-saturation and dark swatches, and overlapping pairs (about 140 renders, read in 16-bit ProPhoto). The evidence chose the color space: a Hue shift leaves HSV saturation and value of linear ProPhoto RGB unchanged to 0.001, and Saturation and Luminance shifts leave the hue and the other quantity unchanged, so Camera Raw works in HSV of linear ProPhoto RGB, where the color mixer works, not in an Oklab-like space. The luminance range is on HSV value, not luminance: a blue as bright in value as an orange is selected alike.

**Rendering.** `crates/rawmakase-engine/src/develop/point_color.rs` applies each swatch to linear ProPhoto RGB after the color mixer. A pixel's weight is the product of a hue, a saturation and a luminance weight, each a trapezoid with smoothed ramps (smoothstep raised to fitted powers, rising ramps steeper for saturation and luminance), faded out below saturation 0.058. The hue window is ±0.72 rad at Range 50; Range scales distances from the swatch by 2^(k · (0.5 − Range)) with separate k per dimension below and above 50 (Range 0 narrows saturation most, Range 100 widens it a little). Within the selection, Hue turns the hue by up to 35° at ±100, Saturation multiplies saturation by 1 + 0.9·|x| (divides when negative), Luminance scales value by 2^(1.39·|x| − 0.24·x²), and Variance moves hues away from the swatch (×(1 + Variance)) and scales distances in saturation and encoded value by 2^(0.79·Variance) and 2^(0.21·Variance). Swatches apply in turn, each to the result of the ones before: on a patch selected by three swatches, adding them up overshot Camera Raw by about 0.06 in encoded units, applying them in turn by 0.013. Channels the swatch pushes past white clip there. Black & white renders leave Point Color out, as Camera Raw does. The GPU preview runs the same steps.

**Panel.** The Color Mixer has Lightroom's two tabs, Mixer and Point Color. Point Color's dropper adds a swatch of the color clicked in the photo (as Point Color sees it there: a 5×5 average of the photo rendered as shown, with lens corrections, retouching, masks and the swatches already there) with Lightroom's default ranges and selects it; it refuses colors too neutral to select (saturation below 0.058) or too dark (encoded value below 0.06), colors that already have a swatch, and a ninth swatch, saying why in the status line. Up to eight swatches sit beside the dropper; right-click one to delete it or all of them. The selected swatch shows its sampled color beside the color it turns it into, then Hue Shift, Sat Shift, Lum Shift and Variance (−100 to 100), Range (0–100) and, under Ranges, the Hue, Sat and Lum Range as four handles on a gradient (outer and inner points; the inner points keep the sampled saturation and luminance between them, so Camera Raw keeps the swatch). The sample is taken off the UI thread, once the preview shows the current settings fully rendered. Visualize Range renders the photo as edited, gray wherever the selected swatch selects nothing (applied to the finished color, so no later color control tints it, and never to what range masks select from), in the preview only and not while the dropper is out; the dropper's sample is dropped if the dropper is put away, the Library or Before opens before it arrives. The panel's reset clears both tabs. The dropper is put away when the tab, the Treatment or the module changes. Each gesture is one History step (Point Color: Add Swatch, Delete Swatch, Point Color Hue Shift, Point Color Hue Range and so on), and Copy, Paste, Sync and presets carry the swatches with Color Adjustments, as Lightroom's Color group does. The sliders show the stored values × 100; Lightroom's own panel was not compared.

On a held-out chart (40 renders, different patches from the fit), the operator reproduces Camera Raw to 0.001–0.008 RMS in encoded sRGB, against 0.010–0.048 without it. On the corpus chart (`pc-*` cases, Camera Raw 18.7), mean ΔE00 before (Point Color left out) → after: Hue +50 2.45 → 0.86, Saturation −80 1.81 → 0.83, Blue Luminance −60 1.75 → 0.95, Variance −80 1.31 → 0.98 and +60 1.18 → 0.97, Range 0 1.22 → 0.99 and Range 100 2.12 → 1.26, a swatch across red 1.96 → 1.05, narrow ranges 1.32 → 0.96, overlapping swatches 2.12 → 0.98, eight swatches 4.43 → 1.45, a near-neutral swatch 1.52 → 1.20, Saturation +100 on a saturated magenta 1.57 → 1.18, with the color mixer and grading 2.72 → 1.89; the default render is 0.91. Luminance +50 on saturated oranges stayed at 1.86 → 1.82 (p95 10) while RAWmakase compressed out-of-gamut colors toward gray; RAWmakase now clips them per channel as Camera Raw does, turning them towards yellow (0.76, p95 1.9).

## Targeted adjustment

The Color Mixer's HSL view and the B&W panel have Lightroom's Targeted Adjustment Tool: the target button (beside Adjust, or on each group's heading under All; beside Auto in B&W) or Cmd+Option+Shift+H, S, L (Hue, Saturation, Luminance) or G (B&W) opens it, and the button or Esc puts it away. Drag up or down on the photo: the bands that change the color where the drag started move, the most involved one by 100 per 250 points of travel and the others in proportion, and the panel marks the sliders that are moving. Each drag is one History step named after the most involved slider ("Orange Saturation +12", "Black & White Mix Orange +20"), and it turns the panel on. Space still pans, and Before can't be adjusted.

`develop/targeted.rs` decides how a drag is shared, from the color as each control sees it, not from the screen: `quality::targeted_sample` renders a 5×5 average around the press, as the photo is shown (lens corrections, retouching, masks), and stops at the color mixer's input (linear ProPhoto RGB after the tone curves) and at the Oklab color the hue weights take (after the mixer and Point Color). The sample is taken off the UI thread; what is dragged before it arrives is applied when it does.

- The measured mixer has no band weights of its own, so a band's share is the size of its measured change at ±100 to that color for the channel being adjusted (the tables the slider scales), relative to the largest. A band under a tenth of the largest stays put, so measurement noise in unrelated bands doesn't move them. Tested on a skin tone: Orange leads for Hue, Saturation and Luminance, which is the band that changes it most through the mixer.
- For the B&W mix, the share is the render's own interpolation between the two bands either side of the color's Oklab hue (`hue_weights`).
- A color with Oklab chroma under 0.01 moves nothing, and the status line says so.

Lightroom's own rates, which band it lets lead and its History names were not compared; the 250-point rate and the tenth cut-off are RAWmakase's choices.

## Black & white

**Treatment.** The Basic panel's Treatment switches between Color and Black & White, as in Lightroom; V does the same, and each switch is one History step (Convert to Black & White, Convert to Color). The color mixer's settings and the black & white mix are both kept while the other is in use. Choosing a black & white profile (an XMP look with `ConvertToGrayscale`, such as Adobe Monochrome) converts to Black & White, leaving one for a color profile converts back, and choosing Color while a black & white profile is in use changes to the photo's default profile (Preferences › Raw Defaults). The B&W panel replaces the Color Mixer whenever the photo renders black & white, by its Treatment or by its profile. Copy and Sync carry Treatment with the profile (Treatment & Profile) and the mix with Black & White Mix.

**Auto mix.** As in Lightroom with its preference "Apply auto mix when first converting to black and white" (Preferences › Profiles & Presets, on by default), converting a photo whose mix was never set applies the Auto mix; a mix already set, by hand, by Auto or by an earlier conversion, is kept. A conversion asked for while the photo is still decoding waits for it, unless the edit changes otherwise first; a black & white profile chosen before the photo has decoded converts without the Auto mix. An all-zero mix counts as never set. The B&W panel's **Auto** sets it again as one History step (Black & White Mix: Auto) and is greyed out while the mix is Auto's. Imported edits keep the mixer values Lightroom resolved, with or without `AutoGrayscaleMix`; a preset or sidecar that names Auto without values gets the estimate when the photo is at hand; otherwise the current mix stays, as Auto white balance leaves white balance. Exports write `ConvertToGrayscale` and the mix for a photo that is black & white by its profile too.

How Camera Raw chooses it was measured by asking Camera Raw 18.7 for its Auto mix (a sidecar with `ConvertToGrayscale` and `AutoGrayscaleMix`; Camera Raw stores the mix it resolved) on 146 synthetic DNG scenes of colored stripes: neutral ramps, one or two dominant hues, low and high saturation, dark and bright, two-color scenes and other white balances. Exposure and Saturation do not change it; white balance does (the same scene at 3500 K gets Blue +32 instead of +15). A gray ramp gets Red −9, Orange −19, Yellow −22, Green −27, Aqua −19, Blue +9, Purple +15, Magenta +4, the values Lightroom's catalog shows for real photos converted in it (within 2). The 146 results lie in a three-dimensional family (0.5 slider steps RMS off it), as a mix chosen by red, green and blue weights would. They are predicted by the direction in which the scene's colors vary most: the principal axis of their covariance in linear ProPhoto RGB, as white balance and the profile render them. `crates/rawmakase-engine/src/develop/black_white.rs` measures the mean and covariance of the decoded camera values once per photo (on a grid of about 1024 pixels across, without pixels near clipping), applies the white balance and profile matrix to them, and maps the unit axis to the eight sliders with a linear fit to 122 of the scenes. The others are flat single colors, for which Camera Raw returns a fixed mix of its own; a flat photo gets the gray ramp's mix here. Trained on the rest and tested on 30 held-out scenes, the estimate is off by 1.3 slider steps on average (a constant mix: 5.5), and by at most 10 on any band. On the synthetic chart, which none of the scenes resembles, it gives −8, −18, −23, −27, −17, +12, +18, +6 against Camera Raw's −9, −19, −23, −27, −18, +11, +16, +4. Two-color scenes, where a small change in the colors flips Camera Raw's mix to the opposite end, are predicted worst: up to 30 steps off on a band when left out of the fit.

### Chart gray

The black & white conversion renders from Camera Raw 18.7's own renders of the dense synthetic chart, at a zero mix and with each band at −100, −50, +50 and +100 (`scripts/corpus/color-mixer.py fit-black-white`). Camera Raw's gray at a zero mix is close to the color's luminance (Rec. 709 Y; within about 10%), with blues and purples brighter; −100 on a band takes its saturated colors to black, and darkening is not linear in the slider. RAWmakase keeps the color mixer's grid of hue × saturation × value of linear ProPhoto RGB: a table of log2 gray luminance over the color's at a zero mix, and one per band and measured position, interpolated between positions (and toward no change at 0). The gray's Oklab lightness is the cube root of that luminance; the GPU uses the same grid through the mixer's lookup.

On the chart's patches left out of the fit, the gray's lightness is within mean ΔL* 0.38 of Camera Raw's (worst position 0.78), against 2.23 for the earlier operator. On the corpus chart, mean ΔE00 before → after: zero mix 0.91 → 0.51, Red +60 1.26 → 0.53, Orange +100 1.65 → 0.61, Yellow −60 2.30 → 0.82, Green +60 1.13 → 0.51, Blue −60 2.46 → 0.77, Purple +60 1.49 → 0.60, the Auto mix 2.30 → 1.75. As in Camera Raw, no band moves a gray: each band's table leaves the lowest saturation row unchanged. A monochrome look whose color table pushes colors beyond what the chart covers is a little further off at Amount 200 (2.41 → 2.50), and its p95 rises at 100 and 200.

**Earlier rendering.** Before the chart gray, an operator no longer rendered (edits made with it render with the chart gray) changed Oklab lightness by the hue-weighted sum of the band sliders, times the color's chroma, times 1.78 when brightening and 4.37 when darkening, fitted to Camera Raw 18.7 renders of the synthetic chart with single bands at ±60 and +100 and the Auto mix (`bw-*` cases). Neutrals stay as they are. The earlier operator added a quarter of the slider sum regardless of chroma, which also moved grays (the Auto mix darkened the gray ramp by 2.8 ΔE00). Mean ΔE00 per case, before → after: Auto mix 2.41 → 2.30, Red +60 1.99 → 1.26, Orange +100 2.23 → 1.65, Yellow −60 2.71 → 2.30, Green +60 1.44 → 1.13, Blue −60 3.01 → 2.46, Purple +60 1.83 → 1.49; a mix of zero stays at 0.91, as the color render. Camera Raw darkens strongly saturated blues and yellows much further than this (a −60 takes them nearly to black), which leaves p95 ΔE00 of 10–13 on those cases.
